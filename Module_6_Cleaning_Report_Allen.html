Project vision

Help a career seeker compare career paths by showing how much AI is currently observed across occupations and tasks, while making clear that exposure can reflect assistance or workflow change rather than certain job replacement.

Business question

Which occupations have the highest observed AI exposure, and which work tasks show the greatest AI penetration? The primary comparison is observed_exposure by O*NET occupation; penetration by task is the supporting measure.

08 · LIMITATIONS

What the cleaned data cannot establish

Documented, unresolved constraints

No occupation-to-task link

The task table has no occupation code or task ID. It cannot be joined safely to the occupation table, and it cannot show which listed tasks belong to a specific career.

Duplicate identity is uncertain

Identical visible rows may be redundant copies or distinct upstream O*NET records whose identifiers were omitted. The clean file supports distinct-description analysis; the source supports occurrence-weighted sensitivity checks.

Exposure is not replacement

Observed exposure combines capability and use. It can represent automation, augmentation, or workflow change and is not a forecast that an occupation will disappear.

Career criteria are incomplete

Salary, employment growth, location, education costs, worker preferences, physical presence, soft skills, and regional demand are absent. Career recommendations require auxiliary sources.

Platform and time scope

The measures reflect the release’s methodology and observed Claude usage. Adoption patterns, models, and deployment contexts may change, so later releases may not be directly comparable without version controls.

Similar text is unresolved

The rules do not collapse paraphrases, spelling variants, or capitalization differences. Doing so would require an authoritative task identifier or a documented semantic-matching method.
